Original work, 2026 refresh

Predicting the next NFL play type from strictly causal context

An original scikit-learn pipeline predicts which of six play types comes next — field goal, pass, punt, QB kneel, QB spike, or run — using only information available before the snap plus the previous play's context. The experiment is split chronologically: models are trained on earlier games and measured once on later games they never saw. This page reports that single holdout measurement, plus three small self-contained reference examples. Every number below is read at runtime from the repository's own results/∗.json; nothing is typed in by hand or copied from an external benchmark.

Inputs: 11 numeric features (field position, clock, down and distance, timeouts, score differential, previous-play yards and a clock-running proxy) and 5 categorical features (home/away/posteam/defteam and previous play type).

Protocol: a single date cutoff at –; earlier games train, later games are the holdout. There is no test-set model selection and no random row shuffling.

Disclosure: the 2026 refresh — its code, tests, documentation, results and this page — was produced with AI assistance under Jadon Calvert's direction. The underlying project is his 2024 coursework; the numbered results here are new.

The accuracy leader is not the universal winner

The random forest posts the best accuracy (–) but a lower macro F1 than logistic regression (– vs –). Its rare-class behavior is the reason: on the holdout it caught – of – QB spikes (recall –, F1 –), flagging only two plays as spikes across the two holdout seasons. It was precise about those two and blind to the other 119. Logistic regression caught – of – spikes (recall –, F1 –), at the cost of more false spike flags.

This is the honest headline of the experiment: accuracy and macro F1 disagree, the disagreement is concentrated in the rarest class, and a single-number summary cannot carry that story. Both facts are reported together, and neither model is described as universally best.

Scope, rights and honest limits

Nothing is deployed

  • The forest is the default prediction model, but its probabilities are uncalibrated. They are model scores, not probabilities that should be trusted or acted on.
  • This page is a static report. It is not an inference service, and there is no hosted demo: serving predictions would require honest pre-play input that a web form cannot verify. The repository's predict command exists for local, context-complete input only.
  • No production, betting, or scouting use is claimed or implied.

Historical 2024 scores are not comparable

The author's 2024 coursework reported roughly 0.75–0.77 micro-averaged F1 under a different protocol (a random row-level split that leaks future games into training). Those figures are narrative history, are not reproduced in this repository, and must not be compared with the numbers on this page. The measurements here come from one game-separated chronological holdout, evaluated once.

Data rights are unresolved

The NFL play-by-play source is published on Kaggle with the license field Unknown, and upstream NFL data rights are unresolved. This repository therefore ships no raw rows, no play descriptions, no player identifiers, and no model files — only original code and aggregate derived results. Obtain the data yourself from the Kaggle dataset page; details in docs/data.md and docs/provenance.md.
